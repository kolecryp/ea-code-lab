Before You Trust an EA: A Backtesting Checklist

A backtest is a tool for examining how a trading system would have behaved under specified historical conditions. It is not proof of future profitability.

1. Verify the test setup

  • Confirm the correct symbol and timeframe.
  • Check the test date range and available historical data.
  • Choose an appropriate modelling method for the strategy.
  • Confirm inputs and any external dependencies.

2. Include realistic costs

Review spread assumptions, commissions, swaps, and execution conditions where applicable. A system that looks attractive before costs may behave very differently after costs.

3. Check for overfitting

If you repeatedly tune parameters against the same historical period, you can end up fitting noise rather than a durable effect. Reserve data for out-of-sample evaluation and avoid treating the best parameter combination as automatically trustworthy.

4. Inspect more than net profit

Review drawdown, trade count, average trade, exposure, losing streaks, and how results vary across market conditions. A very small sample of trades provides weak evidence.

5. Look for implementation problems

Check that signals are not using future information, position sizing behaves as intended, and stop-loss or exit logic works in edge cases. Compare logs with expected behaviour.

6. Forward-test cautiously

After historical tests, consider demo forward-testing under realistic conditions. If you ever consider live use, start only after understanding the risks and the possibility of losses.

Remember

Past performance does not guarantee future results. No checklist can eliminate market risk.
